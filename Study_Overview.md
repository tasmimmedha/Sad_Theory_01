### 1. Fundamentals of Systems & Software

* **[1.1 (1) SDLC (Software Development Life Cycle)](file:///d:/Sad_Theory_01/Week_01.md#L25-L34)**: A structured, phased framework (Planning, Analysis, Design, Implementation, Testing, Maintenance) used by analysts to systematically build and deliver reliable information systems.
* **[1.1 (1) Prototyping Model](file:///d:/Sad_Theory_01/Week_01.md#L35-L49)**: An iterative development technique where early working models are rapidly built and evaluated with user feedback to clarify ambiguous requirements before full implementation.
* **[1. (2) Types of Software](file:///d:/Sad_Theory_01/Week_01.md#L52-L66)**: Software categorized by architecture and purpose into System (OS/drivers), Application (Bespoke, COTS, Enterprise), Embedded (hardware-bound), and Web/Cloud platforms.
* **[1. (3) Organization Structure](file:///d:/Sad_Theory_01/Week_01.md#L69-L79)**: The operational hierarchy (Hierarchical, Project-based, Matrix, Flat) that defines decision-making authority, reporting paths, and data access workflows across the enterprise.

### 2. System Planning & Feasibility

* **[2.1 System Initiation & Scope](file:///d:/Sad_Theory_01/Week_01.md#L84-L86)**: The preliminary phase that identifies business problems or opportunities, defines project boundaries, and establishes baseline expectations.
* **[2.2 (i) Types of Feasibility & Applicability to a Scenario](file:///d:/Sad_Theory_01/Week_01.md#L87-L104)**: A multi-criteria assessment evaluating whether a project is viable across Technical (capability), Economic (cost-benefit/ROI), Operational (user acceptance), Schedule (timeline), and Legal/Ethical constraints within a real-world scenario.

### 3. System Modeling & Analysis Diagrams

* **[3.3 (i) Use Case Diagram (with `<<include>>` & `<<extend>>`)](file:///d:/Sad_Theory_01/Week_01.md#L109-L130)**: A behavioral UML diagram mapping system functional goals from the perspective of external actors, where `<<include>>` represents mandatory shared behavior and `<<extend>>` captures optional or conditional flows.
* **[3.3 (ii) Use Case Descriptive Form](file:///d:/Sad_Theory_01/Week_01.md#L132-L148)**: A standardized textual specification documenting a use case's actors, triggers, preconditions, postconditions, main success flows (happy path), and exception handling.
* **[3.3 (iii) Activity Diagram](file:///d:/Sad_Theory_01/Week_01.md#L150-L164)**: A flowchart-like UML behavioral diagram modeling step-by-step procedural workflows, decision logic, and concurrent activities using forks and joins.
* **[3.3 (iv) Data Flow Diagram (DFD)](file:///d:/Sad_Theory_01/Week_01.md#L166-L178)**: A process modeling diagram tracking how data enters from external entities, transforms through numbered processes, stores in repositories, and exits across hierarchical levels (Context Diagram to Level 2).
* **[3.3 (v) Swimlane Diagram](file:///d:/Sad_Theory_01/Week_01.md#L181-L188)**: A cross-functional process diagram partitioned into columns or rows to explicitly map departmental, role-based, or subsystem ownership for every activity in a workflow.
